Help & guide

Quick start

  • 1. Add your managers (Board → “Add manager”, or the “+ Add” button top right). Leave capacity at 100% unless someone is part-time or still ramping.
  • 2. Add projects and build the team inline — pick a Lead (occasionally two), Contributors, and an effort level for each person.
  • 3. Log work items (escalations, DITL requests, one-offs) as they arrive. Leave the owner as “— none —” to park an item in the Backlog until you decide who takes it.
  • 4. Read the Board: each card shows a weighted heat label — who is overloaded, who has room.

Everything saves automatically; there is no Save button. The first time you change something you'll be asked your name once, so the “Last saved by” banner can attribute changes. Cancelling that prompt cancels the change too.

The Board and the manager drawer

The Board shows one card per active manager: weighted workload heat (Light → Overloaded), the numeric score, and counts for projects leading / supporting, escalations (red), DITL, and one-offs. Hover the heat label to see which score range that band covers.

Click any card (or Tab to it and press Enter) to open that manager's drawer: everything they're carrying, their ten most recent wins, and what's rolling off soon. From the drawer you can add a work item (owner pre-filled), assign them to a project, mark items done, edit their details, and copy / export / print a 1:1 summary — built for weekly 1:1s. Print from the drawer prints the drawer alone.

Trend: every save also records each active manager's score for that day (the latest save of the day wins). Cards show a 12-week sparkline once there is more than a day of history, and the drawer shows six months against the heat bands with a line like “Heavy since 2026-07-20 (91 days)”, which also goes into the 1:1 summary. A day with no saves keeps the previous day's score — the score only moves when the data does. The raw score and capacity are stored separately, so changing someone's capacity later doesn't rewrite their past; changing the band thresholds re-reads the whole history against the new bands.

Archiving a manager (Settings) takes them off the board. If they still own open work, you're asked first, and those items then appear under Needs attention as “Owner archived” until you reassign them.

Timeline — who frees up when

The Board answers “who is loaded now”; the Timeline answers “who has room in six weeks”. Each active manager gets a 90-day lane with a marker wherever something they carry is expected to come off:

  • Solid marker — a project role's until (end) date, or a work item's roll-off date.
  • Hollow marker — no end date was set, so the project's target date (for a role) or the item's due date stands in for it.
  • Left gutter (“1 past”) — dates already gone by: things that should have rolled off. Review them under Needs attention. Right gutter — “later” than 90 days, and “no date” for anything with none of those dates.

Under each name, now is the board's score, and 2 wk / 6 wk / 12 wk are projected loads (colored by heat band, capacity included), assuming every dated item comes off on its date and nothing new arrives. Sort by Most room in 6 weeks to see who to hand the next project to. Hover a marker for what it is; click it to open that project or work item, or click a name for the manager's drawer. Projections are a planning aid only — they never change the Board, which keeps counting an item until it is done or archived.

Projects, leads & effort

A project has a status (Active / On hold / Complete), a priority, dates, and an optional Jira epic link. Its team is managed on the project itself: one entry per person with a role (Lead or Contributor) and an effort (Light / Medium / Heavy). A person can appear only once per project; removing someone archives their assignment rather than deleting it, and so does switching a row to a different person (the new person gets a fresh assignment). Each team member can have an optional until date — the assignment's roll-off date.

Most projects have one Lead; a project with two co-leads is fine — mark both as Lead and each scores as a lead.

Only Active projects count toward workload.

Archiving a project is for one that shouldn't be on the list at all — entered by mistake, a duplicate, or cancelled before it started. (Finished work is closed out instead.) An archived project leaves the Projects list and stops counting toward workload, roll-offs, and Needs attention, but its team and related work items are kept: choose Show: Archived on the Projects view and click Restore to bring it back as it was.

What the Projects view lists: by default only current projects — Active and On hold — so the ones that can still need attention aren't buried. Closed-out and archived projects are kept out of sight; pick Closed out, Archived or All from Show to pull them up.

Related work: a work item can name a related project. The project card counts its related work items (click the count to see them in the queue, filtered to that project), + Work item on the card logs one already linked, and the queue, the drawer, and the 1:1 summary show the project beside the item.

Closing a project out: every project card has a Close out button, and a project whose target date has passed also shows up under Needs attention with the same action. Closing out asks for a completion date (today by default, backdate it if you like) and sets the status to Complete — it never requires a start, target, or end date, so open-ended and non-time-bound projects close out the same way. A closed-out project keeps its team and its history and stops counting toward anyone's workload; Reopen puts it back to Active and clears the completion date.

Work items, backlog, roll-off & history

  • Types: Escalation (red), DITL (blue), One-off (amber), Other (gray).
  • Other: pick Other and the form asks what kind of work it is. That free-text label (up to 60 characters) is then shown in place of “Other” everywhere the item appears — queue, board drawer, 1:1 summary, search — and it is searchable. Reports still group all of them under Other, and the Other weight in Settings still applies.
  • Backlog: items with no owner appear in the amber Backlog panel on the Board, so you assign work where everyone's load is visible.
  • Click-through: a manager's name, a project's team member, or a work item's owner is a link — click it to jump straight to that manager, project, or work item, from the drawer, the Projects list, the Work Items table, search, or Needs attention.
  • Done: marking an item Done stamps its completion date and moves it to History; it then feeds the owner's “Recently completed” list in the drawer.
  • Roll-off: when an item's roll-off date (or an assignment's end date) passes, nothing disappears silently — a “Needs attention” chip appears in the nav. Open it to review and archive items deliberately, one by one or all at once. Active projects past their target date appear there too, with a Close out action; assignments on a closed-out project stop appearing, because closing out settles them.
  • History: Work Items → “Show: History” lists everything archived. Nothing is ever hard-deleted.
  • Undo: the most recent archive — a work item, a project, a manager, marking an item Done, or Archive all past roll-off — can be undone with the Undo button on the message bar (or in the Needs attention dialog), until you make another change. Undo puts back only what that action changed; anything edited again since is left as it is.
  • Age: the queue flags items that have been open 14+ days.
  • Reassigning: changing a work item's owner keeps a record of who had it and when. The queue shows “previously …” under the title, the earlier owner's drawer lists it under Handed off for 90 days, and Reports → Work items carried per manager counts it for everyone who owned it during a period — so “how much did Ada carry last quarter” stays answerable after her work moves on.
  • Reports: counts work items by the period they were opened in (all time, year, quarter, month, or ISO week), split by type. The chart shows the 20 most recent periods. Download CSV saves every period at the grouping you've picked — one row per period, oldest first, with Total and a column per type (custom “Other” labels count as Other) — ready to paste into a review deck or spreadsheet.

Reference tickets by ID or short title plus a link — never put sensitive incident details or customer data in this file.

How the workload score works

Counting items alone is useless — a heavy lead role is not a token one-off. Each item contributes a weight (defaults: project lead 3, contributor 1, escalation 4, DITL 2, one-off 1, other 1), and project weights are multiplied by effort (Light ×1, Medium ×1.5, Heavy ×2). Only active projects and open / in-progress items count toward current load.

The total maps to a heat band: 0–4 Light · 5–9 Balanced · 10–14 Heavy · 15+ Overloaded. Every weight, multiplier, and band is editable in Settings — calibrate against your real team after a couple of weeks.

Capacity: the bands are the same for everyone, but people are not — a part-timer, a new hire still ramping, someone with a standing commitment outside this tracker. Each manager has a capacity (Edit manager), as a percentage of a full load: 100 by default. The heat band uses the score divided by capacity, so 6 points at 50% capacity reads as 12 — Heavy, not Balanced. Wherever capacity isn't 100%, the card shows both numbers (“score 12 · raw 6”) and a Capacity chip, so the adjustment is never hidden.

Scores are fractional (effort multiplies them), so a score can land between two whole-number bands — 4.5, say. It belongs to the band below: the highest band it has actually reached. One medium-effort lead role is 3 × 1.5 = 4.5, which is Light, not the next band up. Hover a heat label to see the band's range and the exact score.

Where your data lives (and how sync works)

  • Local first: data is stored in this browser (IndexedDB) and saves automatically. The app opens instantly, with zero prompts, in any modern browser.
  • Team sync (Edge / Chrome): Settings → “Link shared file” points Heimdall at a folder in a synced drive (OneDrive / SharePoint / Google Drive). It keeps a shared heimdall-data.json there, pulls teammates' changes when you return to the tab, and remembers the link across sessions.
  • Conflicts: last write wins, decided by save timestamp. The banner always shows who saved last; “Reload latest” re-reads the shared file on demand.
  • Change log: Settings → Change log lists every saved change field by field — when, who, which record, old → new — newest first, and downloads as CSV. It doesn't prevent a lost edit, but it lets you see what was lost and redo it: when one copy overwrites another, the data is last-write-wins, but the log is merged — the person whose edit was overwritten still sees it in their log, and it reaches everyone else with their next save. The last 500 changes are kept. Everyone sharing a file should run the same Heimdall version — an older copy (before 0.12) drops the log when it saves.
  • Other tabs: two Heimdall tabs or windows in the same browser share one local copy; switching to a tab loads anything newer the other one saved.
  • Backups: synced saves also write a timestamped heimdall-data.backup-…json beside the shared file — at most one every 30 minutes, so the 10 that are kept span hours of work rather than the last ten edits. If the shared file is ever corrupted, Heimdall refuses to overwrite it and offers to restore from a backup.
  • Export / Import: Settings → Export downloads the full dataset as JSON — a manual backup and the sharing route for non-Chromium browsers. Import loads one back in.

Important: clearing this browser's site data erases the local copy. Link a shared file, or Export periodically, so a copy always exists on disk.

Tips & troubleshooting

  • Search (top right) matches managers, projects, and work-item titles, and jumps straight to the right place.
  • “+ Add” creates a manager, project, or work item from anywhere.
  • Firefox / Safari work fully in local mode; live shared-file sync requires Edge or Chrome.
  • If “Reconnect shared file” appears, the browser wants permission confirmed — one click resumes syncing.
  • Escape closes any dialog or menu; Enter confirms the name prompt.
  • Dark mode follows your system setting; Settings → Appearance can force Light or Dark for this browser.
  • Nothing leaves your machine: no network calls, no analytics — a strict Content-Security-Policy blocks them by design.

Do not store sensitive content (incident details, customer data) — reference tickets by ID or short title with an optional link. Data is stored locally in this browser; nothing leaves your machine unless you link a shared file or export it. See the Help tab for full instructions.
